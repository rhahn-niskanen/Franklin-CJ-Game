      Why the two measures disagree

      Crimes averted counts every index crime the same: a stolen car equals a shooting. Property crimes are ~80% of all crime, so options that prevent many thefts and burglaries — cameras, the crime lab, treatment — rise to the top.

      Dollars saved weights each crime by its social cost — about $5K for a larceny, $700K for a nonfatal shooting, $10M for a homicide. Under this measure, anything that prevents shootings dominates. Flip the toggle and watch the points trade places.

      The dotted rays are value-for-money guides: a point's slope from the origin is its effect per dollar, so steeper is more cost-efficient. Estimates are additive central values from the causal evaluation literature, rounded to two significant figures; overlapping programs double-count somewhat, and hollow marks show effects extrapolated from intermediate outcomes. Benefits that are not crimes — overdose deaths averted by treatment, fair process from indigent defense, accountability from body cameras — appear in neither measure.
